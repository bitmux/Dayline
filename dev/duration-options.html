End times & duration

Four ways to put an event's end on the spine. Same three events in each, so they compare directly. Everything is drawn at the real card's tokens and geometry.

The constraint worth deciding first: the spine is ordinal, not proportional. Rows are the same height whether the next event is ten minutes away or six hours. That is deliberate — a proportional day makes a quiet evening a huge blank and a busy hour illegible on a 520px column. It also means duration cannot be encoded as length without lying: a 20-minute row and a 3-hour row would be drawn the same. So duration should be stated, not drawn. Option C is the one exception worth looking at, and it is the one to be most suspicious of.

End time sits under the start, inside the existing 66px gutter. Costs no horizontal room and no extra rows. Collides with the weather line, which already lives there — you would have to pick one, or widen the gutter to ~84px and lose it from the content. Point-in-time entries (sunset) simply have no second line.

Coexists with weather — the gutter stays for when, the content says how long. Duration is the more useful of the two facts most of the time ("how long am I tied up"), and it needs no mental arithmetic. Cost: a second visual object on the title line, and it competes slightly with the sage sentence beneath.

The rail thickens from the dot to an end-cap. Beautiful, wordless, and it lies: both blocks above are the same length, but one event is 30 minutes and the other is 3¼ hours. The eye reads length as magnitude whether or not you meant it to. Include this only if the spine becomes proportional — which is a different card.

Not an alternative — an addition to whichever of A or B you pick. An event that spans now currently falls into the struck-through past the moment it starts, which is wrong: it is the single most relevant row on the card. This keeps it live above the marker, in accent, and answers the only end-time question that is urgent — when does this finish?
