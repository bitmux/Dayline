Brief · page one

Three ways to spend the first screen

Your current page one answers "what is the state of every subsystem." All three directions below answer a different question: "what does this house need from me right now, and what is it about to do without me." Everything that isn't the answer moves off page one.

Trust is the real spec

Slowness, renamed entities and broken automations are why a control stops being touched. So: every control reads back its state in words, every tap shows an immediate pending state, and an entity that stops reporting says so instead of lying.

Nothing static

Cards earn their slot by relevance. The attention band is absent when nothing is wrong. Scenes shown are the ones plausible for this hour and this calendar, not all of them.

Say what it does

No bare scene names. "Goodnight" carries its consequence underneath it. A button nobody can predict is a button that gets avoided.

Assumptions: 810×1180 wall tablet as primary, phone as the same layout in one column. One dashboard for all three of you, adapting to time and calendar rather than to identity — with a small allow-list so Kid's tablet hides the few controls she can't use. Content is your real data from the screenshots.

2Dark timeline, and three ways to carry security

The timeline is one card, dark, built to be reproducible from what you already have: CalDAV plus Google via HA's calendar entities, `sun`, and your automation triggers. Security options follow — all three treat it as awareness, so the resting state is a single quiet line, and cameras only claim space when something actually happened.
